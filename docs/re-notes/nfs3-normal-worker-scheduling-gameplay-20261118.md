## Diagnosed and repaired public-binder preparation

A third, fresh authored Device2 control enabled the existing browser native
guard and completed 60 presents, with three recovered native modules matching
the same frozen module. No physical input was injected. At the checkpoint:

- The native owner was healthy, unbound and nonterminal.
- Its main/focus HWND was 65537, process 1000 and native thread 1; no modal or
  capture was present. The valid scene observation was 46ms old.
- The legacy Direct3D bridge existed and had completed 60 presents.
- The GL bridge and GL endpoint were absent.
- `snapshot().ready` was false and `bind()` refused with
  `browser native owner unavailable or already bound`.

The frozen `lib/browser-native-input.js` identity reads
`wine.hostCtx.getGlGpsBridge()?.endpoint`; `ready()` requires that endpoint.
An absent GL endpoint is therefore sufficient to prevent binding even with
the observed fresh healthy native scene. The actual refusal and source hashes
are retained in `guard-readiness.json` and `guard-source-proof.json` in the run.
This demonstrates failure of the available unchanged guard preparation on a
legacy Device2 owner; it is not an observation of NFS3's producer/consumer
servicing and is not a scheduler defect diagnosis.

The correction adds the actual legacy bridge to identity selection, without
creating a GL bridge. It requires the real d3dim-gps endpoint in the owning
render manager's port map, that manager's Worker/module/memory, the worker
device and the native window selected by the bridge presenter. These references
and the epoch stay pinned after binding. Ambiguous GL/legacy owners, replaced
ports/devices/Workers/modules/memory/windows/epochs, closure and async faults
are rejected. Focused negative tests also retain the timing and USER-actor
fences. Existing GL guard regressions pass.
